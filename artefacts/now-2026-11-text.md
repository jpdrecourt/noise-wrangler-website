# Now

Updated November 2026, from Barreiro, Portugal.

## SMC 2026, Zagreb

Preparing my talk and poster for SMC 2026 in Zagreb, on Saturday 7 November. The talk: "Creating an instrument for a wandering mind and clumsy hands". I'll share the paper once it is published in the proceedings.

## Fuzzy and fussy

Preparing for the conference, I've improved my invention principle: humans are both fuzzy and fussy. Imprecise in what they ask or do, hard to please in what they accept. In French, vague mais exigeant.

## Playable Ideas

Working on Playable Ideas, a collection of small toys to experiment with design strategies. They run on drum sounds for now. I want to move them to concrete sounds and expand the range to other aspects of fuzziness and fussiness.

## Invariance

Listening to Luc Ferrari's Les Anecdotiques gave me the principle of invariants: semantic repetitions induced in the listener. It's what Playable Ideas currently explore. The question remains: what is motif when it's not applied to instrumental sounds?

## Listening criteria

Thinking about music that is satisfactory in the long run. Reading Brian Olewnick's Just Outside, I distilled six criteria: intent, detail, air, eventful, routine, shape. FSOL's Lifeforms still remains incredibly strong thirty years later, and feels quite close to the music the Conductor could make.

## Concrete sounds as the vocabulary

I'm not Chris Watson; I'm more like a beatboxer with concrete sounds. The sounds are the vocabulary, not the subject. They have their own syntax and a strong presence, and they cannot repeat the way drum sounds do.

## Sound reconstruction

Thinking about how to denoise a sound by exploring its semantic components instead of spectral denoising.

## The interface as a cat

Bringing together Trillo and Poliks' critique of transcriptionist interfaces in Interface after AI and Olewnick's line about an instrument "utterly consistent within whatever set of indecipherable rules", to explore how an interface could be like a cat. Cats aren't compliant but they're rarely out of place; they just do their thing. Until now, interfaces tend to be dogs.
