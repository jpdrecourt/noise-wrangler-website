## Fuzzy and fussy

- "Humans are both fuzzy and fussy": imprecise in what they ask, hard to please in what they accept. FR: vague mais exigeant.
- Fuzzy is the input spec, fussy is the acceptance test. The instrument must take the first and pass the second.
- Three years, three versions (Max, C++, Reaper), "none making the music I want to hear". The rig works; what comes out fails *eventful* and *intent*. "An instrument for a music I don't know yet."
- "Randomness isn't surprise, it is a shrug." "Randomness is lack of commitment." Instruments with opinion.
- "The Conductor doesn't listen. I do." You tell the sounds how to act: second person.

While preparing for the conference, I've improved my invention principle: Humans are both fuzzy and fussy - Imprecise in what they ask or do, but hard to please in what they accept. In French, vague mais exigeant.

## Invariance

- "Generalized ostinato" renamed invariant, from Ferrari: foundation, not ground. A voice is an anchor, not a message.
- "A little invariance goes a long way." Structured repetition without a score.
- The invariant is semantic and made by the listener. A strong one carries twenty to thirty minutes; a broken repetition carries an hour.
- Memory must repeat a motif verbatim before it decays or it "doesn't anchor". "How would the audience know we have a motif if we don't repeat it?" "The window is the future memory."
- In the rig: the loop track is the Conductor's invariant, memory its mutating cousin. Loop material must "resist endless looping".
- "What is motif when it's not applied to notes?"

Through listening to Les Anecdotiques, came up with the principle of invariants, semantic repetitions induced in the listener. It's what the Playable ideas are currently exploring. The question remains: What is motif when it's not applied to instrumental sounds.

## Sound reconstruction

- Started in August from one recording: a girl singing in a reverberant corridor, Tate Modern. Twenty-eight versions, the winner by ear.
- The target is the memory, not the signal: "I know the girl, I was there, I remember the corridor."
- A pontoon at Cais do Sodré split into creak, water, floor, passing, room; then recreated from named parts. "The grime is the credential."
- "Probably the most innovative part of my work so far, a really different way of making music." Not shared half-baked; the seed of the next paper.

Thinking about how denoise a sound by exploring its semantics components instead of spectral denoising. (vague on purpose)

## The interface as a cat

- From Olewnick on Krebs and Unami: an instrument "utterly consistent within whatever set of indecipherable rules". The hand is felt without the rules being read.
- "Cats aren't compliant but they're rarely out of place. They just do their thing." Until now the interface has been compliant, like a dog.
- Trillo and Poliks, *Interface After AI*: interfaces after deep learning stay transcriptionist (intention inflexible, one direction) and hypertelic (specialised to the extreme). "What can I do to change that?"
- Where you part from them: "it's not the machine that is brilliant, it's the user able to identify something they like."
- Hibikidō, parked: the instrument's next interface is language, and the space it searches is written by the player. Naming redraws the space without training.

Bringing Trillo and Poliks (link) criticism of transcriptionist interfaces and Olewnick idea of consistence within indecipherable rules (point to the right review) to explore how interface could be like a cat, not compliant but rarely out of place, instead of a dog (compliant).
